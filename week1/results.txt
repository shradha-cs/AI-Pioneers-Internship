Why: duplicates double-count the same customer, which inflates counts,

Columns dropped: ['customerID', 'gender', 'total_charges']
  customerID   -> unique identifier, no predictive meaning (a model could memorise it).
  gender       -> not significant at the 5% level (p = 0.083) AND a protected attribute:
                  dropping it avoids a model that treats customers differently by gender.
  total_charges-> almost fully explained by tenure x monthly_charges (redundant,
                  causes multicollinearity), so the two parent features are kept instead.

Columns KEPT despite a non-significant chi-square result:
  SeniorCitizen  (p = 1.000) and payment_method (p = 0.395)
  Why kept: with only ~1,200 rows a non-significant test is weak evidence of 'no effect',
  and both are plausible business drivers (electronic-check customers often churn more).
  Keeping them costs little: if they truly do not matter, the model gives them
  coefficients near zero. They are re-checked through the model coefficients in Step 12.

Why Min-Max: tenure (1-72), charges (15-101) and support calls (0-6) sit on
very different scales, which would let large-valued columns dominate any
distance- or gradient-based model. Min-Max maps all to 0-1 while keeping the
shape of each distribution, and it is safe here because the extreme errors
were already removed in Step 6 (Min-Max is sensitive to outliers).
